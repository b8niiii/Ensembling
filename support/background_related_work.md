# 2. Background and Related Work

Relation extraction from news combines several challenges: deciding which relations are supported by the available text, handling noisy supervision, and obtaining useful predictions within a limited computational budget. These challenges are closely connected. A more expensive system may recover additional relations, but those recoveries are valuable only if they satisfy the evaluation target and outweigh the errors introduced. This chapter establishes the task and evaluation concepts needed for that comparison, reviews compact relation extractors and ensemble mechanisms, and explains how compression and cost measurement inform the positioning of this thesis.

## 2.1 Relation Extraction: Sentence-Level, Bag-Level, and Document-Level Settings

Relation extraction (RE) identifies semantic relationships between entities in text. A prediction can be represented as a triple \((h,r,t)\), where \(h\) and \(t\) are the head and tail entities and \(r\) is a relation from a specified ontology. The entity ordering matters whenever the relation is directional. For example, a parent-to-child relation cannot be assigned to the reversed pair without changing its meaning. The ontology also determines which relationships are eligible outputs: a text may describe an association that the available label set cannot represent.

RE tasks differ in whether entity mentions are supplied or must also be detected. With supplied entities, the model predicts relations between known mentions or entity pairs. Joint extraction additionally requires locating and typing entities, so errors in detection can prevent an otherwise correct relation from being produced. OpenNRE explicitly distinguishes relation prediction with annotated entities from optional entity recognition and linking. This distinction is essential when interpreting benchmark scores, because a supplied-entity classifier and an end-to-end extractor solve different problems ([Han et al., 2019](https://aclanthology.org/D19-3029/)).

Sentence-level RE uses a sentence as its main evidence unit. A common formulation supplies two mentions and asks the model to classify their relationship. Some benchmarks require one label, while others allow multiple labels or multiple extracted triples. Consequently, sentence-level evaluation does not imply a universal output format. Entity markers, supplied types, candidate-pair restrictions and the presence of negative examples can substantially change the task's difficulty.

Bag-level RE groups textual instances mentioning the same entity pair. Let

\[
B_{h,t}=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)
\]

denote a bag of mention instances for the ordered pair \((h,t)\). Each instance contains its textual evidence and the corresponding mentions. Grouping instances allows a model to recover a relation expressed in one sentence even when other co-mentions are uninformative. This is particularly useful under distant supervision, where a knowledge-base relation may be attached to sentences that do not express it. Aggregation can operate on sentence representations, attention weights or relation scores; these choices determine how evidence contributes to the bag prediction ([Han et al., 2019](https://aclanthology.org/D19-3029/); [Gao et al., 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.findings-acl.112/)).

Document-level RE instead operates over a coherent document containing multiple entities and mentions. A relationship may require combining evidence across sentences, resolving coreference or following intermediate entities. The document preserves a discourse context that a collection of pair co-mentions may not retain. Document-level tasks can still supply entity mentions and coreference chains, as in GLiDRE; document-level extraction therefore should not automatically be equated with joint entity detection ([Armingaud and Besançon, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.00757v2)).

NYT10m, the main benchmark considered in this thesis, belongs to the bag-level setting. Gao et al.'s revised benchmark supports sentence-level annotation and bag-level evaluation, with bag gold obtained by combining the relations annotated in its constituent sentences. It is not a benchmark for extracting all relations from complete New York Times articles. This separates it from DocRED and Re-DocRED, as well as from the Malaysian English news documents studied by Chanthran et al. ([Gao et al., 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.findings-acl.112/); [Chanthran et al., 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-long.216/)).

The thesis adopts a closed-ontology, multilabel formulation with supplied entity spans. For a positive relation inventory \(\mathcal R\), a system predicts

\[
\widehat R(B_{h,t})\subseteq\mathcal R.
\]

Several relations can be supported for the same pair. An empty predicted set represents NA: no positive relation from the ontology is accepted for that bag. NA does not mean that the entities have no relationship in the world. It means that the system does not extract an eligible relationship from the supplied evidence under the chosen decision rule.

These distinctions constrain comparisons throughout the chapter. Results on the original distantly supervised NYT10 are not results on the later manually evaluated NYT10m. Christou and Tsoumakas evaluate NYT11 and convert several datasets into supplied-pair, single-label classification; their scores therefore do not directly describe NYT10m bag prediction. Similarly, published joint-extraction results for GLiNER-Relex cannot be assigned to a local adaptation that bypasses entity detection and aggregates sentence scores. The task definition must remain explicit before differences are attributed to model size or ensemble design ([Christou and Tsoumakas, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22606v1); [Stepanov et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10108v1)).

## 2.2 Distant Supervision and Manual Evaluation

Distant supervision addresses the scarcity of manually labelled RE training data by aligning text with a knowledge base. If the knowledge base contains a relation between two entities, sentences mentioning those entities can be used to construct training examples. Mintz et al. demonstrated this approach by aligning Freebase relations with Wikipedia co-mentions and training a classifier from lexical and syntactic features. The method made large-scale supervision possible without annotating every training sentence ([Mintz et al., 2009](https://aclanthology.org/P09-1113/)).

The alignment introduces two different forms of noise. First, a relation may hold between the entities but remain unexpressed in a particular sentence. A sentence mentioning a person and a city, for example, need not describe the person's birthplace even if that city is their birthplace. Second, knowledge-base incompleteness can leave a textually expressed relationship unlabelled. An absent edge is therefore an imperfect negative label. Bag-level methods partially address the first problem by pooling multiple mentions, but they do not eliminate incorrect assignments or missing relations.

Manual evaluation is needed because agreement with distant labels and agreement with textual evidence are different outcomes. Gao et al. show that evaluation protocols can change the ranking of RE systems. Their manual reannotation asks annotators which relations are expressed in the sentences, and their bag-level reference is formed by taking the union of the sentence annotations. For a bag containing instances \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\), this gives

\[
R(B_{h,t})=\bigcup_{j=1}^{n}R(x_j).
\]

A bag is positive for a relation if at least one constituent instance supports it; it is NA when no positive relation remains. This combines annotated sentence evidence rather than introducing unrestricted deductions from the fact that the entities co-occur ([Gao et al., 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.findings-acl.112/), Section 5.2).

The central methodological distinction is between factual correctness and extraction correctness. In a fact-discovery task, the question is whether a proposed relation holds between the entities. In text-grounded extraction, the question is whether the supplied text supports that relation. Mintz et al.'s human evaluation of newly discovered relations is closer to the former target, whereas Gao et al.'s manual protocol follows the latter. Zhang et al. also discuss cases where a language model relies on internal knowledge to select a relation that is not sufficiently supported by the document. These are different evaluation objectives rather than contradictory accounts of the same objective ([Mintz et al., 2009](https://aclanthology.org/P09-1113/), Section 7; [Gao et al., 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.findings-acl.112/), Section 5.3; [Zhang et al., 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.319/), Appendix F).

This thesis uses textual support as its primary manual criterion while allowing motivated inference. A supported relation need not appear through the literal wording of its label: apposition, coreference, demonyms and contextual role interpretation can express relational information indirectly. Inferred positives must nevertheless identify their textual premises and the step connecting those premises to the ontology label. World-knowledge-only correctness is recorded separately when a necessary premise is absent from the supplied evidence.

Some inferences are pragmatic rather than logically necessary. Interpreting a sentence as national-team representation can support a nationality annotation under a declared domain convention, but it does not amount to an explicit legal statement of citizenship. That convention cannot be extended automatically to playing for a club, coaching a team or working in a country. Likewise, geographic containment requires the appropriate spatial evidence and head-to-tail direction; a city–country association alone does not establish capital status. The reviewed papers do not prescribe a universal boundary for all such cases. Consistency therefore requires defining the accepted inference conventions before using the resulting annotations to compare systems.

Manual reference quality also depends on the annotation and sampling process. Gao et al. use independent annotations, majority decisions and adjudication. Their resource includes model-selected examples among the originally NA sentences, which helps reveal missing relations but is not a uniform sample of negative cases. Similarly, deliberately selected development examples are valuable for diagnosing errors, yet their results cannot be treated as representative final-test estimates. Distant labels, manually reviewed development diagnostics and an independent final evaluation must retain their separate roles ([Gao et al., 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.findings-acl.112/), Section 3.1).

Finally, textual support is an evaluation property, not a direct observation of the model's internal process. A supported prediction may have been reached partly through memorized knowledge, while an unsupported prediction may happen to be true. The annotation procedure assesses the relationship between the output and the available evidence. It does not claim to determine whether the model internally recalled, inferred or guessed the answer.

## 2.3 Small Language Models and Encoder-Based Relation Extractors

The term small language model does not identify a single parameter threshold across the reviewed literature. This thesis uses an operational sub-billion definition for its main local members: fewer than one billion parameters, including the components required to produce their predictions. For a dual-encoder system, both encoders count towards this scale. Quantization can reduce stored weight size without reducing the number of learned parameters, so parameter count and memory footprint must be reported separately.

Scale descriptions in ensemble papers require particular care. Ensemble SuperICL combines sub-billion members with an 8B final decoder; its complete prediction pipeline is therefore larger than its small-model pool. COSMosFL studies models between 7B and 12B, while the biomedical ensemble of Li et al. contains T0pp with 11B parameters. These studies provide relevant evidence about mechanisms and specialization, but they do not establish the feasibility or performance of a wholly sub-billion local RE ensemble ([Mojarradi et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21868v2); [Cho et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/LLM4Code66737.2025.00007); [Li et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocae061)).

Encoder-based relation extractors compute contextual representations of text and entity mentions, then use task heads to predict relation scores. Generative systems instead produce labels or structured outputs through autoregressive decoding. Both approaches can be specialized, and encoder–decoder architectures form another distinct family. OpenNRE illustrates the encoder approach with convolutional and BERT-based models. Generative prediction offers flexible output formats, but requires a procedure for mapping generated answers to valid relation IDs; format validity remains separate from semantic correctness ([Han et al., 2019](https://aclanthology.org/D19-3029/)).

Model size alone does not explain RE performance. General pretraining, domain-specific pretraining, synthetic extraction supervision and target-task fine-tuning produce different forms of specialization. Christou and Tsoumakas report an average positive-class micro-F1 of 0.828 across seven processed general-domain benchmarks for a task-tuned Qwen2.5-0.5B configuration with two demonstrations. Their GPT-5.4 comparison obtains 0.693 with zero-shot API prompting. The contrast supports the effectiveness of specialization under those conditions, but differs in supervision and demonstration availability. Their RoBERTa-base control reaches 0.826, further motivating a strong encoder baseline rather than assuming that autoregressive generation is preferable. The study is a preprint and its NYT11/pair-classification protocol differs from the thesis's NYT10m setting ([Christou and Tsoumakas, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22606v1), Tables 4 and 11).

GLiNER-Relex is an encoder framework for joint entity and relation extraction using textual label descriptions. A shared bidirectional encoder contextualizes entity labels, relation labels and the input text. Entity spans are represented and scored, while ordered head/tail representations feed a relation-pair network. The pair representation is matched to relation-label representations to produce independent scores, allowing multiple relations to be selected. The ordering of the pair encodes direction. Its published experiments evaluate joint extraction on several datasets, with synthetic multitask supervision preceding evaluation ([Stepanov et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10108v1)).

For this thesis, supplying the benchmark's exact entity spans bypasses GLiNER-Relex's detection stage. Sentence-level scores are then aggregated into bag predictions. This adaptation removes one source of error but introduces a different input and aggregation protocol, so its quality must be measured directly. Published results also require cautious reading because some numerical comparisons differ between the paper's table and surrounding prose, and its detailed large-model training recipe does not fully establish the recipe of every released variant.

GLiDRE version 2 uses separate encoders for document text and relation labels. It pools supplied mentions and coreference information into entity representations, constructs ordered pair representations, and scores their compatibility with label embeddings. Approximately 800M parameters refers to the combined system. The model is evaluated under synthetic-pretraining-only, low-resource, episodic and fully supervised regimes. These regimes are consequential: on Re-DocRED test data, the paper reports 17.32% micro-F1 in its zero-shot setting and 77.83% after supervised adaptation. A released pretraining-only checkpoint therefore cannot be assigned the supervised score ([Armingaud and Besançon, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.00757v2), Tables 3–4).

Together, these studies motivate compact specialized extractors while making training provenance a necessary part of the comparison. Using a frozen released checkpoint avoids new local fine-tuning, but it does not imply that the model is unspecialized or that its original preparation was free. Training-source overlap, supplied entity information, ontology compatibility and adaptation regime must be documented before published performance is used to interpret local results.

## 2.4 Language Model Ensembles and Error Complementarity

An ensemble combines information from multiple predictors to obtain a final decision. Dietterich identifies statistical, computational and representational reasons why this can help: several hypotheses may fit limited training data, optimization may find different solutions, and a combination may express a decision boundary unavailable to one member. These explanations motivate accurate predictors with differently located errors. They do not guarantee that adding members improves every metric or that disagreement alone is useful ([Dietterich, 2000](https://web.engr.oregonstate.edu/~tgd/publications/mcs-ensembles.pdf)).

The reviewed language-model systems combine predictions at different stages. Those stages determine what information is available to the combiner and what costs must be counted.

| Mechanism | Information combined | Representative evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Output voting | Final labels, triples or ranked candidates | COSMosFL; biomedical ensembles; Lu et al. |
| Score fusion | Aligned model scores before the final decision | DEFENSE; Zhang et al. |
| Context fusion | Predictions supplied to another model through its prompt | Ensemble SuperICL; SLCoLM |
| Routing or selective refinement | Decisions about which inputs require another prediction stage | Zhang et al. |
| Joint decoding | Model distributions combined during generation | Chan et al. |

Output voting retains final decisions rather than their full score distributions. In a multilabel RE setting, union accepts any label selected by a member, whereas intersection requires agreement. Union can recover relations missed by one predictor but also admit its false positives. Intersection can remove unsupported additions while discarding correct relations found by only one member. These are different precision–recall policies; neither is universally preferable. COSMosFL combines ranked fault-localization votes, and Li et al. use majority aggregation in biomedical extraction. Their output structures and metrics differ from NYT10m, even though the aggregation principle is relevant ([Cho et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/LLM4Code66737.2025.00007); [Li et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocae061)).

Score fusion preserves information about how strongly each member supports a label, but requires aligned labels and interpretable score scales. A sigmoid output or a maximum vote share is not automatically a calibrated probability. Different members can produce scores with different distributions even when their rankings are useful. DEFENSE combines successive compact-encoder snapshots with fixed weights after incremental self-training. It provides evidence for snapshot aggregation on fake-news classification, although inconsistencies in its published uncertainty-weighting specification limit exact reconstruction of that component ([Folino et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.asoc.2025.112794)).

Context fusion asks another model to interpret member predictions. Ensemble SuperICL supplies labels and confidence scores from several specialized models alongside demonstrations and the query. The final decoder produces the class decision, so this is not a simple vote or arithmetic average. Its reported confidence scores are not accompanied by a demonstrated calibration procedure. Tang et al.'s SLCoLM similarly supplies a specialized extractor's predictions to larger models, then merges their triples. One merge variant replaces predictions for weaker relation types rather than taking an unrestricted union. These methods involve an additional decoder and therefore cannot be evaluated as though their cost were only that of the small predictor pool ([Mojarradi et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21868v2); [Tang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ipm.2025.104286)).

Selective refinement changes which inputs reach an expensive stage. Zhang et al. use a trained document extractor to identify difficult entity-pair decisions, obtain LLM scores for candidate relations, fuse the scores and perform a verification query. Their selector operates at the entity-pair level; it does not independently choose a different model for every relation label. Temperature-based score balancing also does not, by itself, demonstrate probability calibration. This work is direct evidence for a hybrid DocRE pipeline, while routing proposals in COSMosFL remain possible extensions rather than evaluated components ([Zhang et al., 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.319/)).

Joint decoding combines model distributions before complete answers exist. Chan et al. distinguish globally defined distributions over strings from locally renormalized next-token combinations and use sequential Monte Carlo to approximate global ensembles. The theoretical scope matters. For fixed convex mixture weights, \(P=\sum_i w_iP_i\), the expected value of a fixed correctness indicator \(a(y)\) satisfies

\[
\mathbb E_{P}[a(y)]
=\sum_iw_i\mathbb E_{P_i}[a(y)]
\leq\max_i\mathbb E_{P_i}[a(y)].
\]

This elementary mixture bound does not establish an accuracy ceiling for majority voting, argmax decisions, nonlinear score fusion or input-dependent selection. Chan et al.'s generation experiments also use substantially larger models and incur particle-sampling and tokenizer-alignment costs. Their results should therefore inform mechanism distinctions rather than predict the performance of a compact static RE vote ([Chan et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05432v1)).

Member construction creates another distinction. Different pretrained checkpoints can vary in architecture and supervision; snapshots share a training trajectory; precision variants share the same underlying weights before quantization. Lu et al. evaluate voting across whole-model copies at different bit widths. This motivates testing precision-induced changes, but does not establish independent errors or net savings for encoder RE. Interacting agents form a further category: different roles, memories and messages can change behavior even when all agents reuse one checkpoint. The multi-agent survey of Li et al. provides workflow terminology, not evidence that such interaction improves a static NYT10m ensemble ([Lu et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22483v1); [Li et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s44336-024-00009-2)).

Useful complementarity must be assessed against reference labels. Let \(P_i\) be the positive bag–relation facts predicted by member \(i\), and let \(G\) be the reference fact set. Exclusive correct predictions and exclusive incorrect additions are respectively

\[
(P_i\setminus P_j)\cap G,
\qquad
(P_i\setminus P_j)\setminus G.
\]

The first set represents recoveries potentially available from member \(i\); the second represents additional errors it can introduce. Shared true positives and shared misses complete the picture. A union of only the correct member predictions is an oracle diagnostic, because a deployable combiner does not know which predictions are correct. Actual gains depend on whether the fusion rule preserves useful recoveries while controlling errors.

The empirical literature supports this conditional view. COSMosFL does not surpass the best single model in top-one accuracy under an equal total-call budget. The biomedical system's NER ensemble scores below its strongest individual configuration. In Tang et al.'s main table, collaboration improves macro-F1 while remaining below SpERT's micro-F1. Lu et al.'s precision vote also fails to exceed the best precision copy in every evaluated dimension. These results make the strongest relevant single predictor an essential comparator and motivate evaluating quality together with the cost of every member.

## 2.5 Model Compression: Quantization and Pruning

Compression can expand the set of models that fit a deployment budget, but several mechanisms must be distinguished. Quantization changes numerical representation; pruning removes weights or architectural components. Both can alter predictive behavior, and both may require calibration or adaptation. A smaller stored checkpoint is only one potential outcome: reduced peak memory, lower latency and lower energy depend on the implementation and workload.

Quantization maps floating-point values to a limited set of numerical levels. A simplified symmetric quantizer can be written as

\[
q=\operatorname{clip}\left(\operatorname{round}(x/s),-M,M\right),
\qquad \widehat x=sq,
\]

where \(s\) is the scale and \(M\) depends on the bit width. Weight, activation, bias and accumulator precision need not be identical. Some operators can remain floating point even when most weights are quantized. For this reason, an eight-bit model description must specify which tensors and operations actually use that representation ([Zafrir et al., 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.06188v2)).

Post-training quantization (PTQ) starts from an already trained model, often using calibration examples to choose scales or clipping ranges. Dynamic quantization computes relevant activation scales during inference. Quantization-aware training (QAT) simulates quantization during parameter adaptation so that the model can respond to rounding and clipping effects. Q8BERT compares task-fine-tuned floating-point BERT, dynamic quantization and eight-bit QAT. On SQuAD v1.1 development data, Table 1 reports F1 scores of 88.46, 80.02 and 87.74 respectively. This supports adaptation to quantization error in that setup, rather than a universal claim of lossless quantization or a direct RE result ([Zafrir et al., 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.06188v2)).

Low activation precision is particularly sensitive to large numerical outliers. Outlier Suppression identifies learned LayerNorm scaling and token-dependent activation ranges as important sources of this problem. Gamma Migration moves scaling into subsequent transformations while preserving the full-precision function; Token-Wise Clipping searches for ranges that reduce downstream reconstruction error. The distinction between PTQ, QAT and distillation-assisted QAT is consequential: the paper's four-bit BERT GLUE aggregate is 81.13 without knowledge distillation and 83.56 with it, compared with 83.83 at full precision. Near-full-precision quality therefore depends on the stated preparation procedure, and the aggregate combines task-specific metrics ([Wei et al., 2022](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/6f6db140de9c9f111b12ef8a216320a9-Abstract-Conference.html), Table 5).

AWQ and GPTQ provide additional PTQ background, primarily from generative-model compression. AWQ uses calibration activations to identify important channels and choose weight scaling. GPTQ reduces layer-output reconstruction error through approximate second-order information and rounding-error compensation. Both are weight-only methods: activations informing calibration do not imply that activations are also quantized to the same bit width. Their reported decoder acceleration further depends on runtime and kernel support, so transfer to a DeBERTa-based RE pipeline requires separate compatibility and quality measurements ([Lin et al., 2024](https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/42a452cbafa9dd64e9ba4aa95cc1ef21-Abstract-Conference.html); [Frantar et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.17323v2)).

Pruning differs in its granularity. Unstructured pruning masks individual weights, producing sparse matrices without necessarily changing their dimensions. Movement Pruning learns importance scores during task fine-tuning, using first-order information about how pretrained weights change. At high sparsity, it outperforms magnitude pruning on the examined BERT tasks. However, its reported remaining-weight fractions exclude embeddings, and its supplementary discussion warns that zeros stored in ordinary dense PyTorch matrices do not provide significant inference acceleration. Sparse weight counts therefore cannot be substituted for measured runtime ([Sanh et al., 2020](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/eae15aabaa768ae4a5993a8a4f4fa6e4-Abstract.html)).

Structured pruning removes computation units such as layers, attention heads and hidden dimensions. CoFi jointly learns masks at several granularities and uses distillation and recovery fine-tuning to maintain quality. It supplies concrete latency evidence: a highly compressed MNLI configuration achieves a reported 12.1-fold speedup while accuracy falls from 84.8% to 80.6%. The measurement uses a V100, batch size 128 and sequence length 128; it does not establish the same acceleration at batch one on a local CPU. Its parameter-reduction denominator also excludes embeddings, and the final model depends on task-specific preparation ([Xia et al., 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.acl-long.107/)).

These encoder studies establish relevant compression mechanisms on classification, question answering and related NLP tasks. They do not directly establish preserved NYT10m extraction quality or useful diversity among compressed RE members. For this thesis, each variant must first be evaluated as an individual predictor, with its calibration, recovery and deployment costs recorded. Only then can combining variants be assessed against the strongest compressed single. Compression and ensembling are separate interventions whose joint benefit remains an empirical question.

## 2.6 Quality–Cost Evaluation

The value of an ensemble depends on both the relations it predicts and the resources required to predict them. Green AI argues that efficiency should be evaluated alongside accuracy and that the cost of developing, training and running systems should be made explicit. This perspective is appropriate for RE under local resource constraints, where a modest quality gain may require substantially more inference work ([Schwartz et al., 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.10597v3)).

For multilabel bag-level evaluation, true positives, false positives and false negatives count positive bag–relation decisions. Precision, recall and F1 are

\[
P=\frac{TP}{TP+FP},\qquad
R=\frac{TP}{TP+FN},\qquad
F_1=\frac{2TP}{2TP+FP+FN}.
\]

Micro-F1 pools these counts across bags and relation labels; macro-F1 averages label-specific F1 values. The former emphasizes overall extracted-fact performance, while the latter exposes behavior on less frequent relations. Correct NA predictions do not add positive true positives, although incorrect positive predictions on NA bags contribute false positives. Zero-denominator conventions and per-relation support must be explicit. Gao et al.'s analysis of imbalance and Tang et al.'s macro/micro tradeoff illustrate why one aggregate score is insufficient ([Gao et al., 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.findings-acl.112/); [Tang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ipm.2025.104286)).

Metrics from adjacent tasks cannot be treated as interchangeable. The biomedical study uses a modified Jaccard score, COSMosFL reports fault-localization acc@k, and Chan et al. estimate probability mass assigned to correct generated strings. IgnF1 in document RE adjusts for training-fact overlap under the benchmark's convention; it does not isolate all genuinely unseen reasoning or remove every source of pretraining contamination. Comparisons must retain the source metric and evaluation unit.

Operating-point selection is equally important. Thresholds, member choices, prompts, aggregation rules and evidence-selection limits should be selected on development data and frozen before final evaluation. Gao et al. report the best micro-F1 on test precision–recall curves, a useful published convention but different from evaluating a deployable threshold chosen in advance. In this thesis, distant-supervision validation scores and manually reviewed development diagnostics remain separate references; repeated use of the latter does not turn them into an independent final test.

A static ensemble's total computational work includes every member and the fusion step. For an additive work or expenditure measure,

\[
C_{\mathrm{ensemble}}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}C_i+C_{\mathrm{fusion}}.
\]

Parallel execution can shorten elapsed time without eliminating member work. Its memory requirements can also increase because several models and their buffers are resident simultaneously. Sequential execution changes that tradeoff. Cached predictions make subsequent offline fusion inexpensive, but the deployed system still needs to generate those member predictions. Consequently, elapsed time, throughput, peak memory and monetary expenditure should be treated as distinct dimensions rather than collapsed into parameter count.

Costs also belong to different lifecycle phases. Original pretraining, synthetic annotation, local calibration, pruning or distillation, configuration search and final inference must be distinguished. COSMosFL measures execution time, tokens and GPU energy; CoFi measures hardware-specific speedups and reports task-preparation cost. In contrast, Christou and Tsoumakas explicitly describe their CPU/GPU latency figures as estimates. The contrast illustrates why measured, estimated and absent costs must remain identifiable ([Cho et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/LLM4Code66737.2025.00007); [Xia et al., 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.acl-long.107/); [Christou and Tsoumakas, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22606v1)).

Hybrid pipelines require full accounting as well. Chanthran et al.'s BERT scoring depends on LLM-generated descriptions and hypernyms, whose calls contribute to deployment cost. Zhang et al. show that selective refinement can reduce expensive calls, but their training-plus-inference time denominator cannot be interpreted as inference-only overhead. GLiNER-Relex and GLiDRE provide workload-specific GPU/API comparisons whose hardware, entity inputs and adaptation conditions differ from local bag inference. These measurements motivate instrumentation, not direct numerical projections onto the thesis environment.

Quality–cost tradeoffs can be summarized through Pareto comparisons. A configuration is dominated if another achieves no worse quality and no greater cost, with at least one strict improvement. Separate quality–latency, quality–memory and quality–expenditure comparisons avoid assuming a universal exchange rate between F1 and resources. Measurements should use consistent inputs and document hardware, software, precision, batch size, evidence length and loading conditions. API comparisons additionally require model/provider identification, dates, token accounting and retry costs. Uncertainty analyses must identify whether variation comes from sampled bags, training seeds, demonstrations or repeated execution. The central comparison is with the strongest development-selected single predictor, not merely a convenient untuned member.

## 2.7 Research Gap and Positioning of This Thesis

The reviewed literature supplies complementary pieces of evidence. Gao et al. establish the importance of manually evaluated, text-supported bag-level RE. Compact-model studies show that specialization can be effective, while GLiNER-Relex and GLiDRE provide encoder architectures that can be adapted to supplied entities and textual labels. RE collaborations demonstrate that different models can recover different relations, but their settings include biomedical novelty, coherent documents or historical Chinese joint extraction. Ensemble studies on other tasks and encoder-compression studies provide mechanisms and cost lessons under different scales and workloads.

Within this reviewed corpus, these strands do not jointly establish the cost-effectiveness of a static ensemble of sub-billion RE members on the thesis's NYT10m supplied-pair bag protocol. In particular, published gains do not simultaneously resolve text-supported evaluation, total-member local inference cost, comparisons with a strong single predictor and an API baseline, and the effect of reduced variants. This is a gap in the examined evidence, rather than an exhaustive claim that no related study exists elsewhere.

The thesis is positioned as an empirical investigation of when combining compact relation extractors is worthwhile. Its main comparison uses released predictors under a common input, ontology and evidence-aggregation contract. Original model specialization is documented separately from local adaptation. Ensemble construction must be justified by observed recovery and error patterns, while deployment cost includes every member required by the final rule. The evaluation is designed to compare these configurations with both a competitive individual model and a documented API system.

Three questions organize that investigation:

1. Under a shared NYT10m evaluation protocol, does a static compact-model ensemble improve extraction quality over the strongest development-selected single predictor?
2. Are its additional correct recoveries sufficient to offset newly introduced errors and the full latency, memory or expenditure of its members?
3. When compression is feasible, does it improve the quality–cost tradeoff of individual models or ensembles relative to their unreduced counterparts and the API comparator?

These questions permit negative results. An ensemble that adds false positives, loses correct predictions through excessive agreement requirements or occupies a dominated cost position provides evidence about the limits of combination. If a single compact extractor offers the most useful tradeoff, selecting it is consistent with the research objective. Likewise, reduced variants may preserve quality without generating helpful complementarity. Neither ensemble superiority nor beneficial precision-induced diversity is assumed in advance.

In-context learning and routing remain possible extensions. Demonstrations or member predictions can be supplied in context, as in Ensemble SuperICL and SLCoLM, but retrieval, longer prompts and a final decoder add costs. Christou and Tsoumakas also show that demonstration effects depend on the training/inference format. Episodic support-set fine-tuning, as evaluated by GLiDRE, is parameter adaptation and should be distinguished from frozen-model ICL. Selective routing could avoid unnecessary expensive calls, following the motivation of Zhang et al., but requires development-selected referral criteria and complete accounting for the selector and verification stages. These mechanisms extend the static comparison rather than supplying evidence that it will succeed.

The resulting contribution is bounded and reproducible: evaluate compact RE models and their combinations under an explicit evidence policy, separate direct benchmark evidence from adjacent precedents, and measure whether quality improvements justify deployment costs. The following methodology chapter specifies the dataset roles, model provenance, supplied-span adaptation, bag aggregation, fusion rules, selection procedure and resource measurements needed to answer those questions.
