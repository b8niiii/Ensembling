# Thesis working notes

Updated 8 October 2026. This file records the current scope, protocol, evidence, and open decisions. Pilot tables and settings are consolidated in [Preliminary_experiments.ipynb](../source/Preliminary_experiments.ipynb); paper summaries remain in [literature.md](literature.md).

## 1. Research question and scope

**When does combining small language models improve news relation extraction enough to justify the extra computation, compared with individual small models and a large model accessed through an API?**

The agreed question is retained. The current study focuses on released, primarily encoder-based relation extractors under or around one billion parameters, static ensembles, and feasible reductions of a leading checkpoint. An API LLM remains an external quality–cost comparator; it is not the only relevant baseline. Every ensemble must also be compared with its strongest individual member under competitive development-selected settings.

The 1 October supervision discussion prioritised quantization/pruning and ensembling of reduced variants. On 6 October, the execution order was revised: first establish a released-SLM/static-ensemble versus API-LLM comparison; reduction follows as a separately declared phase. GLiNER-relex large is the **provisional parent checkpoint** for that branch. Quantization changes numerical precision and may reduce memory; it does not reduce the number of parameters. Pruning requires an implementation that actually reduces executable work or storage before an efficiency gain can be claimed. No random-forest analogy establishes error diversity or savings automatically. Five variants and “different splits” remain hypotheses whose construction must be specified, not requirements for the first feasibility test.

ICL, an encoder-informed decoder, routing/cascades, and new task fine-tuning are deferred extensions. A two-member system is acceptable; an additional vote is useful only if its errors and cost improve the measured frontier.

## 2. Dataset and EDA

NYT10m is a news **bag-level** relation extraction benchmark, distributed through [OpenNRE](https://github.com/thunlp/OpenNRE#data) and described by [Gao et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2021.findings-acl.112/). A record contains sentence text, head and tail entity names/IDs/half-open character spans, and a relation label. A bag groups records with the same **ordered** `(h.id, t.id)` within one split. A record count and a bag count describe different units.

| Split | Sentence records | Ordered-pair bags | Role |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Train | 417,893 | 255,526 | Optional future adaptation or train-only demonstrations/calibration inputs |
| Validation | 46,422 | 36,266 | Development, error analysis, thresholds, ensemble and reduction decisions |
| Official test | 11,086 serialized label rows | 5,174 | Completed first evaluation of the frozen comparison; 9,744 distinct sentence/mention units |

Train and validation use noisy distant supervision, not confirmed sentence-level gold. The verified official manual test release stores human labels in **`relation`**, repeating an exact sentence/mention record for each positive label; it contains no `anno_relation_list` field. This corrects the earlier assumption based on OpenNRE's alternative loader format. The field's meaning depends on the verified split/release: train/validation `relation` remains distant supervision. `NA` is an empty positive set, not a positive relation. Bags can support multiple distinct positive relations. Historical validation EDA preserves source rows; the test protocol reconstructs unique sentence/mention units from the repeated manual-label rows before applying its cap.

The provisional cap is 20 sentence records per bag. In validation it shortens **75/36,266 bags (0.21%)**, supplies **44,751/46,422 records**, and omits 1,671 records from model inputs. Selection is deterministic, evenly spaced in original record order, and includes the first/last records. It uses neither labels nor model scores. The dataset itself is unchanged. A paired cap sensitivity check remains open for the affected long bags.

The first frozen official-test comparison is complete and was verified on 8 October 2026. Limited earlier input-overlap and aggregate label-convention audits remain recorded as provenance; they were not model-selection runs. Test outcomes are evaluation evidence, not inputs for revising thresholds, prompts or fusion. Further reduction design and selection must use training/validation and acknowledge prior use of the test.

## 3. Active supplied-entity protocol

The completed experiments use local **GLiNER-relex base, GLiNER-relex large, and GLiDRE large**, sequential CPU inference, the same supplied sentence records, and the dataset's exact head → tail character spans. Entity recognition, entity confidence thresholds, and predicted-name filtering are bypassed. The adapters retain the pretrained span/relation layers and refine model word boundaries at supplied entity offsets when needed; sentence text is unchanged. Offline checks passed for all three checkpoints on all 44,751 selected records; 320 records required boundary refinement.

Every selected sentence stores scores for all 24 directed positive relations. A bag score is the maximum over its selected sentences. A relation is accepted when its score is **strictly greater than** the cutoff. Historical validation reporting started at 0.5 and examined cached 0.7/0.9 diagnostics without new inference. The completed test uses the development-selected frozen cutoffs: large alone at 0.7; majority with base at 0.5, large at 0.7 and GLiDRE at 0.7. Scores are model confidences, not established calibrated probabilities.

Dataset, source, checkpoint, settings, and selected-input hashes remain tied to their runs. `run_extended_gliner_relex.py` remains unchanged because it provides the frozen relation descriptions imported by the active workflow. The active notebook is [Full_validation_relex.ipynb](../source/Full_validation_relex.ipynb). The three-model extension has completed successfully. NYT10m train-seen/unseen cohorts are omitted because membership in that benchmark split does not establish the released checkpoints' actual training exposure. Model cards lack a full checkpoint-specific training inventory; the framework paper describes FineWeb-derived synthetic training but does not certify absence of overlap with NYT10m.

Precision = TP/(TP+FP); recall = TP/(TP+FN); micro-F1 = 2TP/(2TP+FP+FN), pooling positive label decisions across bags. True negatives do not contribute to positive-label F1. The scorer is deterministic evaluation code, not a model. A five-case constructed scorer check was previously passed; the retained adapter tests additionally cover direction, boundaries, multiple relations, and strict cutoff semantics.
